The short version

Relic is a puzzle game, not a data vacuum.

Your progress lives on your phone. There is no account, no sign-up, and we never ask for your name, email, phone number, contacts, photos, camera, microphone or location.

We do use Google services to show ads, catch crashes, and learn which puzzles are too hard. The details are below, and no archaeology degree is required.

About us
🏷️

Who we are

Relic is made by Clever Corner ("we", "us"). This policy covers the Relic app for Android and nothing else.

When it says "you", it means the person holding the phone and holding the brush.

Data on your device
📱

What stays on your phone

Relic keeps a save file in the app's private storage. It holds:

  • Which plots you have restored and which expeditions are unlocked
  • Your coins, hearts and hints
  • Your Daily Dig streak and calendar
  • Your ad-free status and any ad-free time left
  • Your settings, such as theme and reminders
  • When you last played, so reminders know when to stay quiet
  • Whether you have already been asked about notifications or a review

This file is not sent to us. We never see it.

If Android backup is switched on for your device, Android may copy the file to your Google account so a new phone can pick up where you left off. That copy is handled by Google under Google's own terms.

Data that leaves your device
📊

Usage and crash reports

We use Google Firebase to understand how Relic is played and to fix it when it breaks.

Firebase Analytics records anonymous events, for example:

  • Which screens are opened
  • Puzzles started and finished, how long they took, mistakes made, and hearts and hints used
  • Coins earned and spent
  • Whether an ad was shown or watched
  • Settings you chose, such as theme or ads on or off, and whether you own ad-free

Firebase Crashlytics records a crash report when the app falls over: what the app was doing, your device model, Android version and app version.

These reports are tied to a random ID that Firebase creates for the app on your device, never to your name or email. We use them to fix bugs and to make puzzles fairer.

Relic does not have an in-app switch for analytics. If you would rather not take part, the way out is to uninstall the app.

📢

Ads

Relic is free because of ads. We use Google AdMob for a banner on the map and puzzle screens, a video between levels, and optional videos you can watch for rewards.

To serve and measure ads, AdMob may collect your device's advertising ID, IP address (and the rough location it implies), device and Android details, and how you interact with ads. Ads can be personalised unless you limit that. We do not control or receive that data. Google's policies describe it.

While you are ad-free, Relic shows no ads.

To take control, open Android Settings, then Google, then Ads. There you can reset your advertising ID or opt out of personalised ads.

⚙️

Google Play services

A few Google Play features are built in. Google runs them, and Google's privacy policy covers what they collect.

🎮 Play Games
Signing in is optional and only happens if you choose it. Google shows its own consent screen first.
💳 Play Billing
The one-time ad-free purchase goes through Google Play. Your payment details go to Google. We never see your card number.
⭐ In-App Review & Update
Google shows its own sheets asking for a rating or offering a new version.
🔔

Reminders

Dig reminders are notifications that your phone makes by itself. There is no server and no push service behind them, and nothing about you is sent anywhere to produce one.

Relic sets one quiet alarm on your phone, and when it goes off the app looks at your save file and decides whether a reminder is worth sending. At most one appears a day, and they stop altogether if you do not come back.

On Android 13 and newer, Relic asks permission once. You can turn reminders off any time with the Dig reminders switch in Settings or in Android's notification settings.

🧩

Our other creations

Settings lists our other apps. So the list can stay fresh without an app update, Relic asks Firebase Remote Config for it when you open the app. That is an ordinary internet request: Google receives your IP address, the random Firebase ID described above and basic device details. Nothing about how you play is sent with it.

The icons are downloaded from a web address in that list, so the server hosting them also sees your IP address, as any website would. Tapping an app opens its page in Google Play. We do not see what you do there.

Retention, rights & security
🗓️

How long data is kept

Your save file stays on your phone until you clear the app's data or uninstall Relic. Then it is gone, and since we never had a copy, we cannot bring it back.

Analytics, crash reports and ad data are kept by Google for a limited time under its retention settings.

👦

Young diggers

Relic is made for a general audience. It is not designed for children under 13, or under 16 where the law sets that age, and we do not knowingly collect personal information from them.

If you think a child has shared personal information with us, tell us and we will sort it out.

🔒

Keeping things safe

Your save file sits in the app's private storage, which Android walls off from other apps. Data that travels to Google's services is protected by Google, including encryption in transit. No system is perfectly secure, and we would not be honest if we promised otherwise.

Around the world

Google may process the data described above in countries other than yours, including the United States, using the safeguards Google puts in place for those transfers.
